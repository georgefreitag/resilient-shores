Example: the Eden family story

After reading Resilient Shores, one member of the Eden family filled in the story board to think through facing climate change across generations. Here’s their worksheet.

Your story plot

Values:Wants to enjoy grandchildren.
Conflicts:Grandchildren will not want or be able to visit.
Resolutions:Stay as long as attractive to grandchildren. Property not considered an investment.
What:Grandchildren visit / flooding increasing
Where:Troy Beach
Who:Grandparents / grandchildren
When:Threshold crossed. Less attractive to children.
Why:Increased risk
How:Aging children / climate change

After reading Resilient Shores, one Eden family member couldn’t stop dreaming up future experiences with grandchildren. Stories had them enjoying grandchildren in the disaster-prone community. Moving when no longer attractive to children.

Opportunities/Risks embrace alternative obstacles and outcomes – grandparents passing on, grandchildren growing up, or climate change making staying too expensive or frightening.

Some stories had family members being better prepared to bounce back, others included mitigation, and still others involved moving to a new, safer location similarly attractive to their grandchildren.

Epilogue

The Edens lived through Hurricane Apollo and subsequent flooding events, reducing vulnerabilities where practicable after each. They established a risk tolerance threshold: when water surrounded their house monthly, they would move. Their grandchildren grew up before this tipping point was crossed. They gifted their property to the City and moved in with their children. (See the WOOP process.)
