My spouse and I spent years wishing/dreaming about sharing our waterfront home with future grandchildren. We lived in a disaster-prone community, but we believed there would always be time to adapt. part2: >- With climate change, storms grew stronger. Floodwaters reached our yard, insurance costs increased, and summer heat became difficult to ignore. Conflicts with what we valued became obvious. We began thinking about the obstacles and outcomes. Would our grandchildren still want to visit if the house became unsafe or uncomfortable? part3: >- After each event, we focused on bouncing back. With the next storm, we tried mitigation measures to improve flood protection and make our home more resilient. We added air conditioning. Eventually, we crossed our threshold: if flooding became a regular part of life, we would consider moving.
…The End! epilogue: >- We lived through Hurricane Apollo and subsequent flooding events, reducing vulnerabilities where practical after each disaster. We established risk tolerance thresholds: if floodwaters surrounded our home monthly or summer temperatures regularly reached 110°F, we would move. Fortunately, our grandchildren enjoyed Troy Beach throughout their childhood before either threshold was crossed. When the time came, we gifted our property to the City and moved in with our children. arc: top1: "Fun place for" top2: "grand kids" curve_top: "Too Hot" curve_bottom1: "Endured" curve_bottom2: "Until …" bottom1: "Move to a" bottom2: "funner area" ---
