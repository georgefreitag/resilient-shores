About the author

Bob Freitag is a resilience practitioner, educator, and former Director of the Institute for Hazards Mitigation Planning and Research at the University of Washington. A long-time instructor in the Department of Urban Design and Planning, he received the department’s Outstanding Part-time Instructor Award and the Association of State Floodplain Managers’ Jerry Louthain Award for Distinguished Service.

Freitag has written widely on hazards mitigation and floodplain management, developing courses for the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) and the University of Washington. His publications include Floodplain Management: A New Approach for a New Era (Island Press, 2008) and Resilient Shores: Adapting to a Changing Climate Through Storytelling (Springer Nature, 2026).

Before joining the University of Washington, Freitag served with FEMA, deploying on more than fifty Federal Disaster Declarations. His final role with FEMA Region 10 was as a Federal Coordinating Officer, overseeing disaster response operations. Earlier in his career, he worked with architectural and engineering firms in Hawaii and Australia and taught science as a Peace Corps Volunteer in the Philippines.

Freitag holds a Master of Urban Planning degree from the University of Washington.
